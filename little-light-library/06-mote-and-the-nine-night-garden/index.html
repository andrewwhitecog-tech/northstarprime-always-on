Spread 2

The wind snapped MOTE’s lantern dark. MOTE landed beside one mooncap mushroom. “A light can be small,” said the mooncap. One drop of silver dew rolled into the lantern. One light. One step. Into the dark.

Spread 3

On the second night, two green frogs waited by a puddle. “A path can look broken,” said the first. “Until the water holds it together,” said the second. Two round reflections joined the dew. Two lights. Two steps. On we go.

Spread 4

On the third night, three jewel beetles polished a fallen twig. “Why polish a twig?” MOTE asked. “Because we live here,” they said. Three green-blue gleams clicked into the lantern. Three lights. Three steps. The path remembers.

Spread 5

On the fourth night, four pale roots showed through the earth. They did not speak. They held the hill while rain pressed down. MOTE listened with all six feet. Four gold pulses climbed the glass. Four lights. Four steps. Braver now.

Spread 6

On the fifth night, five cloud-flowers folded against the storm. “Are you hiding?” MOTE asked. “We are saving our petals for morning.” Five magenta edges warmed the lantern. Five lights. Five steps. The lantern hums.

Spread 7

On the sixth night, six tiny fish flashed beneath a leaf. They made one quick arrow, then six again. “Together does not mean stuck,” MOTE said. Six blue sparks swam around the wick. Six lights. Six steps. The garden listens.

Spread 8

On the seventh night, seven velvet moths circled an empty shell. They had found a cricket lost in the rain. The library bell rang far away. MOTE was late. MOTE opened the lantern anyway. Seven violet lights became a path home for the cricket.

Spread 9

The lantern went dark again. On the eighth night, eight fireflies found MOTE sitting under a leaf. “I gave the light away,” MOTE said. “Good,” said the fireflies. “Now it knows the way back.” Eight warm lights gathered around MOTE—but did not enter the glass.

Spread 10

On the ninth night, nine night-blooms opened at once. One held silver dew. Two held puddle moons. Three held beetle gleams. Four held root pulses. Five held petal edges. Six held fish sparks. Seven held a cricket’s path. Eight held firefly warmth. The ninth held a place for MOTE.

Spread 11

MOTE set the page-seed in the empty flower. The nine lights did not fill the old lantern. They grew a new one: roots for a handle, dew for glass, a petal door, and a small bright map inside. The garden had remembered every helpful step.

Spread 12

MOTE reached the library after dawn. “You are late,” said the book cart. “I brought directions,” said MOTE. The book cart looked at the living lantern. “That is annoyingly better.” MOTE hung the lantern beside the garden gate. It still shines there for the next traveler— one light, then two, then three, all the way to nine. Can you count the way home?
