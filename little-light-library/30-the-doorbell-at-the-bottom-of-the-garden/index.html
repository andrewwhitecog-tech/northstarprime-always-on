A bell for visitors

Tula hung a little brass bell by the garden gate.

She tied a blue ribbon beneath it.

Now visitors could announce themselves.

She went to arrange the chairs.

Back to contents ↑

02 / 12

Ding

The bell rang.

Tula hurried down the path.

The gate was shut. The lane was empty.

She peered through the slats.

Nobody peered back.

03 / 12

A patient snail

A snail was crossing the path.

'Was that you?'

The snail continued with its crossing.

It was a long way from the bell, and in no hurry to explain.

Back to contents ↑

04 / 12

Again

Tula went back to the chairs.

Ding.

This time she came quietly.

A leaf trembled beside the gate.

The lane was still empty.

Back to contents ↑

05 / 12

Behind the pot

She sat behind a large flowerpot.

Not hidden very well. Her ears showed.

She waited through a bee, two sparrows and one itchy nose.

The bell stayed quiet.

Back to contents ↑

06 / 12

A breeze arrived

The hollyhocks bent.

A stem brushed the blue ribbon.

The ribbon tugged the bell.

Ding.

Tula stood up before the sound had finished.

Back to contents ↑

07 / 12

The visitor without paws

She held the stem away.

The breeze came again.

Leaves rustled. The ribbon fluttered freely.

The bell did not ring.

'Oh,' said Tula. 'You.'

Back to contents ↑

08 / 12

A different place

Tula moved the bell to the other side of the post.

Its ribbon hung clear of the flowers.

She pulled it once.

The sound was exactly the same, but this time she knew who had made it.

Back to contents ↑

09 / 12

Two chairs

She returned to her chairs.

One chair for Tula. One for somebody else.

The garden was pleasant, but rather quiet.

She took the second chair nearer the first.

Back to contents ↑

10 / 12

Across the wall

Pip's ears appeared above the low garden wall.

'What are you doing?'

'Waiting for a visitor,' said Tula.

'I can do that part,' said Pip.

Back to contents ↑

11 / 12

A proper arrival

Pip went around to the gate.

He pulled the blue ribbon.

Ding.

Tula came down the path at a perfectly ordinary speed.

She was smiling before she opened it.

Back to contents ↑

12 / 12

Something to listen to

They sat in the two chairs.

The breeze moved through the hollyhocks.

This time the flowers only rustled.

Pip began a story about a snail.

Tula listened carefully.

The end. Or a beginning.

Talk together

  1. What clues help Tula find what rings the bell?
  2. What changes when she moves the ribbon?
  3. How does Pip know where to find her?

A bell promises a visitor, but Tula's first visitor has no paws. Her investigation turns an accidental sound into a way for a friend to find her.
