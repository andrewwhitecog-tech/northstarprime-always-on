ROAD 01 THE WELCOME LIGHT Vespercoil was a city of seven round terraces turning slowly around a well of stars. Silver roads linked one terrace to the next. Zuri knew every curve. She was eight years old, an apprentice mapmaker, and very proud of drawing straight lines in a city that preferred circles. One evening, the mapkeeper placed a hexagonal lantern in her hands. A single amber star glowed inside. "A new child has arrived on Terrace Seven," she said. "Carry this welcome light there before the last starbell." Zuri tucked the map beneath her coral sash. "One lantern. One road. One easy delivery," she said. The city began to turn.

ROAD 02 WHEN THE ROAD LOOKED UP At the edge of Terrace One, the silver road lifted like a ribbon caught by invisible fingers. Its safety rails folded shut. Its far end pointed straight into the sky. The gravity tide had arrived early. Zuri checked her map. The road on the paper remained perfectly flat. She tilted the page. She tilted her head. She even whispered, "Please." The road did not come down. Behind her, the first starbell rang. Zuri held the lantern close. "A map is supposed to tell me where the road is." An awning keeper nearby smiled. "Sometimes the city waits to see what you will call a road."

ROAD 03 A STAIR OF AWNINGS The awning keeper pulled one brass lever. Saffron shop awnings unfolded across the narrow gap. One touched the next until they made a soft stair in the air. Zuri tested the first canvas step. It held. She crossed slowly, keeping both hands around the lantern. Shopkeepers leaned from their windows and tightened every golden cord before she reached it. On Terrace Two, Zuri drew a saffron curve on her map. "That was not a road," she said. "It was a row of awnings." "Did it help you arrive?" asked the keeper. Zuri looked back at the bright cloth staircase. "Yes," she admitted.

ROAD 04 THE BOOKLIFT The next silver road had curled into a shining loop. Below it, the library's booklift traveled between terraces on two strong cyan cables. The librarian emptied its brass basket and laid a velvet cushion inside. "The lift usually carries stories," she said. "Tonight it can carry the person bringing one." Zuri climbed in. The basket glided over the starwell while books fluttered safely behind crystal windows. On Terrace Three, she drew two cyan lines on the map. "That was not a road. It was a booklift." "Did it help you arrive?" Zuri smiled. "Yes." The second starbell rang.

ROAD 05 THE RISING FOUNTAIN Terrace Three stood below Terrace Four, and every stair between them had turned upside down. In the moon garden, an upward fountain sent great sea-green drops floating toward the higher terrace. The fountain tender placed Zuri and the lantern inside a clear crystal cup. Up they drifted, carried by one enormous shining drop. Zuri could see the whole turning city beneath her boots. She was frightened for one breath and delighted for the next three. On Terrace Four, she drew a sea-green circle. "That was definitely not a road." The fountain tender called from below, "Did it help you arrive?" "It did!"

ROAD 06 ONE MINUTE The road from Terrace Four had folded itself into a knot. The clockkeeper pointed to the enormous violet minute hand sweeping around the tower. "When it reaches the quarter-star, it will touch Terrace Five for exactly one minute." Zuri waited. Waiting felt much longer when she could hear the third starbell preparing to ring. At last, the clock hand aligned. Zuri stepped onto its broad glass surface and walked as it carried her across the gap. On Terrace Five, she drew one violet hand on the map. "A road that lasts one minute is a very unusual road." "But did it help you arrive?" "Exactly in time."

ROAD 07 AROUND BEFORE ACROSS The fifth crossing ran through the public observatory. Its three amber telescope rings had tilted away from one another during the gravity tide. The observatory apprentices turned the first ring sideways, the second downward, and the third almost upside down. For one gleaming moment, the three rings made a winding tunnel from Terrace Five to Terrace Six. Zuri walked around, under, and through. It was the least straight crossing she had ever seen. On Terrace Six, she drew three amber loops. "That road went the wrong direction twice." "Did it help you arrive?" Zuri laughed. "Eventually."

ROAD 08 HAND TO HAND Only one terrace remained, but the next gap was wider than all the others. Mirror polishers stood along its edge. Neighbors waited on balconies beyond. They angled their opal mirrors until a bridge of reflected light reached across the dark. Zuri could not walk on light. But the welcome lantern could travel through it. She opened the lantern's front crystal. The amber star became a warm beam. Mirror to mirror, hand to hand, the light crossed safely to Terrace Seven. Zuri drew six small opal diamonds on her map. Then she looked at the empty lantern in her hands. The light had arrived. She had not.

ROAD 09 NO ALMOST-ROAD Zuri searched the final edge. There were no awnings, no booklift cables, no fountain, no clock hand, and no observatory rings. The mirrors could send light, but they could not carry a child. Across the gap, the newcomer stood beside the amber beam. Their name was Mina. They raised one hand in greeting. The fourth starbell rang. Zuri stared at the six bright marks on her map. Each crossing had begun when someone changed the purpose of something they already had. Perhaps a road was not a thing waiting to be found. Perhaps it was something people agreed to make.

ROAD 10 EVERYONE Zuri lifted the map over her head. "I need one more way!" The answer traveled backward through Vespercoil. The awning keeper sent saffron canvas. The librarian sent cyan cable. The fountain tender sent a crystal cup. The clockkeeper sent a violet gear. The apprentices sent an amber ring. The neighbors turned their opal mirrors. Zuri fitted the six gifts around a round maintenance platform. Reflected starlight filled every piece. The platform unfolded into a glowing patchwork path. It was the seventh road. It was made of six earlier answers and everyone who remembered them.

ROAD 11 THE LIGHT AND MAPMAKER Zuri crossed the patchwork path as the last starbell began. Mina waited beside the amber welcome star. "Your light arrived before you," Mina said. "It found a shorter road," Zuri replied. Together they closed the beam back inside the hexagonal lantern. The single star glowed brighter between their hands. People cheered from all seven terraces. Awnings rippled. Booklift bells chimed. Fountain drops flashed. The great clock turned. Observatory rings shone. Mirrors scattered warm light through the city. Zuri unfolded her map. It no longer looked unfinished. It looked alive.

ROAD 12 ROOM FOR PEOPLE The next morning, Zuri redrew the map of Vespercoil. She kept the silver roads. Beside them she added a saffron curve, two cyan lines, a sea-green circle, one violet hand, three amber loops, six opal diamonds, and a rainbow path made from them all. At the bottom she left a wide blank space. "What goes there?" Mina asked. "The next road we have not imagined yet." From then on, Vespercoil's mapkeepers asked two questions. Where is the road? And if there is none, who can help make one? For a road was not only a line beneath your feet. It was a promise that someone would help you arrive.
