The Real Story: In Andre's Words

Andre is an engineer, builder, programmer, and artist. He builds AI agents, codes generative systems, and spends hours crafting tools for other creators. But in late March 2024, his life shifted abruptly.

The Shock Diagnosis (March 2024):
"I went in for a checkup because I was having problems with calf cramps that would wake me up and cause pain for days, and dry peeling skin on my face, neck, and back. When blood work came back the next day, they advised me to go straight to the ER. I was hospitalized early the next morning.

It seemed completely unreal. They told me I would need surgery to start dialysis and maybe had 2 to 5 years ahead of me in the best-case scenario."

Andre initially chose Peritoneal Dialysis (PD), hoping to treat himself at home. The reality was harrowing: severe abdominal bloating, being unable to digest normally for weeks, living on smoothies, and dealing with nightly cycler alarms that prevented sleep. Most debilitating of all, PD was not clearing enough toxins from his blood, leaving him with relentless, agonizing uremic itching.

In December 2025, Andre underwent a fistulagram in his left upper arm to create permanent vascular access and transitioned to hemodialysis. After beginning on the grueling early morning shift (5:00 AM – 10:00 AM), he recently transitioned to the night shift: 6:00 PM to 1:30 AM at DaVita Meridian Park (with care coordinated across Providence West Side and Kaiser Sunnyside).

Life in the Clinic Chair:
"In the chair, I sleep, I do tai chi with my feet, I watch cartoons. I hold my bandages. It sucks when you’re restless and not tired, but otherwise it’s nice and quiet.

The only part I really hate is that sometimes at the end of treatment, my blood pressure will drop dangerously low if I’m too tired or haven’t eaten well. It feels like falling or drowning in slow motion. And the diet can be brutal because cooking at home when you’re exhausted is tedious, and I love foods like tomatoes, avocados, potatoes, chocolate, and soda—everything that renal patients are forbidden from having."

Despite chronic anemia and exhaustion, Andre refuses to stop creating. He channeled his health journey into building RenalShield, a software tool designed to help other renal patients navigate confusing nutritional labels and lab values.

The Family in His Corner:
"The Darm family has been integral to every aspect of my life. Lauren and Bob especially are amazing, and Jerry, Ryan, Tyler, and their families are all lovely people. Lauren is by my side day in and day out, no matter what happens."

The Immediate Hurdle: Dental Clearance ($4,500)

Most people don't know the single most common reason kidney failure patients get delayed from being placed on the active transplant waiting list: mandatory dental clearance.

Why Dental Surgery is Mandatory for Transplant Listing:
When a patient receives a new kidney, doctors prescribe powerful lifelong immunosuppressive medications to prevent organ rejection. Under immunosuppression, any hidden dental infection, impacted tooth, or deep cavity can seed bacteria directly into the bloodstream—causing lethal sepsis or immediate loss of the new kidney. Transplant hospitals (Providence, Kaiser, OHSU) strictly mandate 100% complete dental clearance before active listing.

Andre currently has impacted teeth, missing teeth, and multiple cavities requiring urgent oral surgery before the transplant committee can clear him. Because standard health insurance and Medicare provide almost zero coverage for major oral surgery, this $4,500 out-of-pocket cost is the #1 physical barrier standing between Andre and a new kidney.

Become a Living Kidney Donor

A living donor kidney lasts almost twice as long as a deceased donor organ and can be scheduled before severe dialysis complications arise. You do not need to be a family member or direct blood match.

1. You Only Need One Kidney

Humans are born with two kidneys but only need one to live a full, normal, athletic life with zero medications.

2. 100% Medical Cost Coverage

All donor screening, medical workup, surgery, hospital stay, and follow-up are fully covered by recipient insurance. Donors pay nothing.

3. National Paired Exchange (No Match Needed)

If your blood type doesn't match Andre, the National Kidney Registry swaps donors in a chain so both recipients receive perfectly matched kidneys.

4. Donor Shield Wage & Travel Protection

Living donors are protected through Donor Shield, including lost wage reimbursement, travel expenses, and legal job protections.
