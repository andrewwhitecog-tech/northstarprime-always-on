ASH AND THE BELL THAT NEEDED TWO PAWS Every morning, Ash rang the Cat Sea bell. BONG meant wake up, harbor. BONG-BONG meant boats may go. BONG-BONG-BONG meant Marmalade is sitting on the rope again. Marmalade did not consider this official.

Ash kept the bell polished. Ash kept the rope coiled. Ash kept the morning exactly where it belonged. When he rang, gold lines woke beneath the streets and blue routes opened across the water. Ash liked being the first sound everyone heard.

Then Luma arrived on the noon ferry. She was a small pearl-gray kitten with seven glass bells in a wooden box. Her bells chimed when rain touched them. ting for a drop, tang for a puddle, talingaling for a tail splashing where it should not. Everyone gathered close.

“Play them again!” called the bakers. “Again!” called the boat cats. Even the gulls stopped pretending to work. Ash polished the great bell harder. No one heard polishing. Ash pulled the rope. BONG. Every window looked his way. That felt better. For one breath.

Spread 5

The next morning, Luma set her little bells near the tide steps. Ash saw the crowd waiting. He pulled once. BONG. The crowd clapped. He pulled twice. BONG-BONG. The boats pushed away. Then Ash pulled with all four paws. BONG-BONG-BONG-BONG-BONG!

The sound struck the Loomwheel under the harbor. Gold streets twisted into knots. Blue boat routes curled into circles. Nine morning reflections slipped out of the water and hid beneath nine different doors. Marmalade floated past the same fish shop three times. “I have seen today’s cod,” he said.

The harbor cats searched for the cause. “Perhaps the glass bells confused the routes,” someone said. Luma closed her wooden box. Ash looked at the great bell. One black-silver paw mark crossed its gold rim. It was his. The easiest answer stood beside him. The true answer sat inside his chest.

Spread 8

Ash climbed onto the bell rail. “Luma’s bells did not tangle the harbor,” he said. His voice came out smaller than a raindrop. “I rang too hard. I wanted the whole morning to sound like me.” The wet curve of the bell held Ash’s eye. It warmed amber, then violet. The harbor waited.

Luma opened her box. “A large bell can call a whole harbor,” she said. Ash lowered his ears. “A small bell can hear which street is lost,” Luma continued. She placed one glass bell beside the great rope. “Can yours call?” “Can yours listen?” Ash asked. Luma smiled.

Spread 10

Ash rang one deep note. BONG. Luma answered with one bright note. ting. Deep called. Bright listened. They played the pattern nine times. Behind each door, one morning reflection lifted its head and followed the sound home.

The gold streets loosened. The blue routes straightened. The boats reached somewhere new. Marmalade reached the fish shop a fourth time, but this time it was because he wanted to. The harbor cheered. Ash heard Luma’s small bell inside the cheering. It did not make his bell smaller. It made the morning wider.

The next day, Ash bolted a second stool beside the rope. One stool had a black-silver paw mark. One had a rainbow-glass paw mark. “Ready?” asked Ash. “Listening,” said Luma. BONG. ting. The Cat Sea morning opened both eyes. And somewhere under the harbor, the Loomwheel learned a new way to turn.
