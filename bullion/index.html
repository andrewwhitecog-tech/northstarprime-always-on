Listen to that. That tone is six thousand hertz. It cannot be faked with tungsten, and it cannot be forged with lead. In three thousand years of human civilizations rising and crumbling, silver has never gone to zero.

You walk down Main Street into five different coin shops. Do you walk out with ten thousand dollars in cash?

Not even close. One shop will offer you nine thousand six hundred. The next might hand you ten thousand one hundred and fifty. And the pawn shop down the boulevard will shamelessly try to hand you seventy-five hundred and tell you they are doing you a favor.

Here is the secret coin shops rarely put on the front marquee: a coin shop is not a storage vault, and it is not an investment fund. It is a logistics turning gear.
When a dealer buys your three hundred ounces, he has two choices. He can put them in his showcase to sell to the next collector walking in tomorrow, or he can box them up and wholesale them to a mega-distributor like APMEX or Dillon Gage.
If he sells to a wholesaler, the wholesaler pays him wholesale bid. That means on generic buffalo rounds, the dealer's guaranteed floor is twenty-five to fifty cents under spot. If he offers you seventy-five cents under spot, he is locking in a twenty-five cent per ounce margin with zero risk. That is fifty to seventy-five dollars of pure profit for five minutes of paperwork.

When you don't know the difference between an American Silver Eagle and a generic buffalo round, you let the shop treat sovereign bullion like refinery scrap.

If a shop offers you spot minus a dollar for an Eagle, do not argue. Do not get angry. Simply smile, zip up your bag, and walk out. A sleek brushed-silver graphic table animates smoothly on screen as Julian enumerates each rule:

Rule 1: Always check live spot 60 seconds before stepping in. Metal prices tick every second on the COMEX. Never rely on yesterday's closing print.

Rule 2: Never clean your coins. Never polish a vintage Morgan dollar or peace dollar. Cleaning removes the original mint luster and instantly destroys numismatic premium.

Rule 3: Separate your stack by category before you arrive. Keep your government sovereign coins separate from generic rounds, and keep your 90% pre-1965 junk silver counted by face value.

Rule 4: Ask for payment terms upfront. Legitimate dealers pay immediate check or cash on sums under five thousand dollars, and official cashier's check or bank wire on larger sums. Never accept post-dated checks or consignment promises.

The Counter Playbook: 5 Tactics Every Stacker Needs

Practical guidance for fathers, grandfathers, and everyday savers visiting a local bullion dealer.

01

The Sigma Verification Test

Always ask the dealer to verify your bullion using a Sigma Metalytics electromagnetic bridge in front of you. A true Sigma test verifies the core of the coin in 0.5 seconds without acid, scratching, or damaging the finish.

02

The 90% Junk Silver Multiplier

Pre-1965 US dimes, quarters, and half-dollars contain 0.715 troy ounces of pure silver per $1.00 of face value. When spot is $32, $1.00 face value has a melt value of $22.88 (a 22.9x multiplier). Never sell junk silver at a flat coin count.

03

IRS Form 1099-B Rules

Dealers are NOT required to report sales of American Silver Eagles to the IRS on Form 1099-B regardless of quantity. For 1 oz generic rounds and 100 oz bars, reporting is only triggered when you sell more than 1,000 total ounces in a single 24-hour transaction.

04

Cash vs. Official Check

Most coin shops carry $3,000 to $5,000 in physical cash on premise for safety. For a $10,000 transaction, a legitimate dealer will issue an immediate official business or cashier's check drawn on a local commercial bank branch.
