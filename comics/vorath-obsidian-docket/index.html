Chapter 1: Concrete & Static

District 9 Steam Tunnels // Lower Reach Subterranean Biometric Auction

The rain in District 9 condenses directly out of the smog at three hundred feet, dropping like liquid solder onto corrugated zinc roofs. Deep in the subterranean vaults beneath the Old Municipal Pier, illegal chrono-arbitrage cartels trade stolen telomere replenishment from honest shift workers. Uncle Vorath arrives armed not with conventional firepower, but with a four-inch slab of matte-black optical obsidian and an unyielding cross-border maritime lien.

Act I: The Three-Degree Corridor

The rain in District 9 didn't fall from the clouds; it condensed directly out of the smog at three hundred feet and dropped like liquid solder onto the corrugated zinc roofs of the Lower Reach. It was sixty-two degrees Fahrenheit outside, but down in the steam tunnels beneath the Old Municipal Pier, the temperature sat at an unvarying, biological seventy-eight. The exact ambient threshold for human tissue preservation.

Uncle Vorath adjusted the lapels of his heavy wool balmacaan. The fabric was damp, smelling faintly of sulfur and cold riverbed. His right hand stayed inside the slash pocket, fingertips resting against the milled titanium chassis of an affidavit seal \u2014 heavy, octagonal, loaded with twenty-four grain compressed nitrogen charges. In the non-linear jurisdictions, a filed injunction wasn't paperwork. It was ballistics.

Beside him, Julian Mercer \u2014 second-year associate, pulse rate currently clicking at 114 BPM on the optical sensor in his collar \u2014 stepped around a puddle of hydraulic fluid.

"The docket says they're trading lot forty-two at midnight," Mercer whispered. His teeth were chattering despite the tunnel heat. "Chrono-Securitization Trust 11-B. That's eighteen years of biological surplus from the Portland dockworkers' pension pool. Vorath, that's an entire generation's worth of Saturdays."

"Keep your chin down," Vorath said. His voice had the dry, gravel drag of an ungreased winch. "And stop measuring theft in metaphors. They aren't stealing their Saturdays, Mercer. They're siphoning cellular telomere replenishment during REM cycle intervals, routing it through offshore sub-meridian cooling arrays, and selling the fractional yield to hedge desks in Geneva as synthetic longevity warrants. Call it what it is on the record, or don't stand behind the bar."

They reached the blast bulkhead at the end of Corridor Seven. Above the lintel, a rusted brass plate read: COLUMBIA HYDRAULIC & COLD STORAGE \u2014 EST. 1894. The stencil beneath it was much newer, spray-painted in luminous cobalt: AUCTION DESK \u2014 CLEARED ENTITIES ONLY.

Two men stood flanking the reinforced iron hatch. Neither wore uniforms. They wore tailored slate-gray stormcoats cut wide across the lats to clear sub-dermal recoil dampers. The one on the left had a mechanical chronometer riveted directly to the radial bone of his left forearm, its sweep second hand stuttering in three-beat micro-bursts.

"Docket closed for the night, counselor," the left one said without looking up. "Private liquidation."

Vorath didn't slow his stride. When he was four feet away, his hand came out of the balmacaan, not with a weapon, but with a four-inch slab of matte-black optical obsidian encased in brushed palladium. He struck the metal frame against the iron hatch with a flat, resounding crack.

"Superior Tribunal of the Cascadia Basin, Admiralty and Temporal Division," Vorath said. His tone was as flat as a concrete slab. "Emergency Writs of Sequestration, Docket 904-Omega. I am court-appointed receiver over all disputed cellular reserves credited to Chrono-Securitization Trust 11-B. You have fifteen seconds to cycle the pneumatic lock before I discharge this seal into the hinge pins and log your refusal as willful contempt with prejudice."

The guard on the left tensed, his right hand twitching toward the stormcoat vent. His partner caught his elbow. The partner recognized the seal.

"That's Vorath," the partner muttered under his breath, voice tightening. "He's not a private eye, you idiot. He's got Article Five standing. If he logs the seal, the maritime receivers seize the offshore nodes at sunrise."

The iron wheels spun. Compressed air hissed out of the perimeter seals like exhaled breath. The hatch swung back on copper-bronze hinges.

Codex Legal Note: Article Five Admiralty Jurisdiction

Under the Cascadia Non-Linear Maritime Compact, biological cargo extracted or transported across tidal estuaries falls directly under Admiralty Receivership. The obsidian seal creates an immutable cryptographic lien that invalidates off-market derivative transactions.
Act II: The Floor

The interior was thirty feet tall, carved out of the original basalt foundation of the waterfront. What had once been an ammonia ice locker was now an amphitheater of tiered mahogany benches illuminated entirely by warm sodium floodlights and the flickering, icy cyan glare of seventy-two vertical terminal screens.

In the center pit, where butchered beef once hung from ceiling rails, eight glass cryogenic canisters stood upright inside a circular bronze dais. Each canister was six feet tall, insulated by vacuum sleeves, containing twenty liters of hyper-dense, golden-amber fluorocarbon suspension. Inside the fluid, suspended in zero-g micro-currents, faint filaments of pale bioluminescence coiled and uncoiled like living sea-glass.

"The reserves," Mercer murmured, pressing a handkerchief to his nose. The air smelled of ozone, isopropyl alcohol, and ancient wet stone.

On the auction podium stood Donald Vance. Fifty-five years old, but wearing the crisp, taut thirty-year-old epidermis that sixty million dollars in illegal chrono-arbitrage could buy in Singapore. His suit was dark vicu\u00f1a wool, his collar fastened with a raw Cascadia river-pearl pin.

"...lot forty-two stands at forty-eight thousand SpaceCash per five-year block," Vance was saying into the goose-neck microphone. His voice echoed off the damp basalt arches. "Bid is with the private syndicate from Lausanne. Going once..."

Vorath walked down the center carpet runner, his leather soles clapping rhythmically against the flagstones. The room didn't erupt; it went instantly, unnervingly silent. Twenty brokers in raincoats and bespoke overcoats turned in their seats, their faces illuminated only by the glow of their handheld bidding tablets.

"Bidding is stayed," Vorath announced, his voice easily carrying through the cold amphitheater. He stepped to the edge of the bronze dais, three feet from the first canister. "Under Rule 44-B, the underlying assets are contaminated by fraudulent extraction."

Vance slowly lowered his gavel. A thin, cold smile appeared across his perfectly smoothed jawline. "Vorath. I wondered when the county would send its favorite dinosaur. You're out of jurisdiction, counselor. This facility sits three feet past the high-water datum line. Admiralty doesn't reach the dry dock."

"It does when the cargo was transported via tidewater pipeline through the mouth of the Columbia," Vorath replied. He laid the obsidian slab directly onto the rim of the bronze dais. A tiny green diode on the palladium casing flickered from amber to solid emerald. "I tracked the flow sensors at four o'clock this morning. Your offshore compressor barge discharged into the river conduit at 0340 hours. That makes this an in-transit maritime seizure. Every canister on this floor belongs to the Admiralty Marshal."

Vance didn't flinch. He leaned forward over the podium. "Do you know who owns the debt instruments collateralized by these canisters, Vorath? You think you're protecting dockworkers? Half the municipal pension board in this city cleared their own heart surgeries using swap derivatives tied to this exact batch. You bust this auction, and by sunrise three judges in the Third District won't have the biological reserve to survive their morning commute."

Mercer swallowed hard behind Vorath. "Vorath... is that true?"

Vorath didn't turn around. His dark eyes remained fixed on Vance. "The court does not trade in hostage math, Vance. You forged the consent riders on forty-eight thousand employee health benefit forms while they were asleep in shift barracks. You turned thirty years of honest labor into speculative paper."

Vance stepped down from the podium. Two private security operatives in heavy tactical rigs materialized from the shadows near the refrigeration pumps, submachine guns slung across their tactical vests.

"You aren't walking out of here with an injunction, counselor," Vance said softly. "You aren't even walking out with your coat."

Act III: The Ruling

Vorath reached down to his right lapel. Slowly, deliberately, he pulled an archaic pair of tortoiseshell reading spectacles from his vest pocket and perched them on his broken nose. Then he took out a thick sheaf of cream-colored legal parchment, bound at the top with lead wire.

"I anticipated your jurisdictional objection," Vorath said calmly. "Which is why I didn't come to argue."

He touched a knurled dial on the side of the obsidian slab. The nitrogen charge inside hissed, and a high-frequency acoustic chime echoed off the basalt ceiling. In the far corner of the room, behind the heavy steel refrigeration ducting, four men in heavy black stormcoats stepped out into the light. Behind them walked two uniformed federal marshals carrying heavy bolt-cutters and red wax security seals.

"I didn't file the seizure order in the Third District, Vance," Vorath said, turning the parchment page. "I filed it at 0800 yesterday with the Supreme Admiralty Council in Victoria. The cross-border maritime lien was granted five hours ago. The marshals aren't here to shut down the auction."

Vorath looked up over his spectacles. The green-gold tritium glow from the nearby canisters reflected off the cold glass lenses.

"They're here to impound you."

Vance's smooth skin paled, the illusion of youth fracturing as the veins in his temple pulsed with unmanaged blood pressure. "You're crazy. The market contagion \u2014 "

"The market can choke on its own bad debt," Vorath said. He picked up his obsidian seal, tapped it twice on the podium rail, and motioned to Mercer.

"Log the canisters, associate," Vorath said, turning his back on Vance and walking toward the exit. "All eight of them. And Mercer \u2014 don't spill a drop. Some welder in Astoria is going to need every second of it."
