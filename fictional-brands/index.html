Brands That Only Exist On-Screen

Prop departments hate lawsuits, so they invent brands — and then reuse them. Over decades the same fake airline, the same fake cigarettes, the same fake tools drift from film to film, show to show, quietly stitching unrelated stories into one accidental universe. This is the real-world cousin of the Continuity Atlas: connection not by plot, but by prop. Tap a brand for its trail.

Why it happens: no real airline wants its name on a crashing plane, no real tobacco company will license a villain's cigarette — so studios keep a stable of "clearable" fake brands. Once a prop exists, it's cheaper to reuse. Fans notice. The reuse becomes an in-joke, then a tradition, then a secret map. Oceanic is the patron saint of the phenomenon.
