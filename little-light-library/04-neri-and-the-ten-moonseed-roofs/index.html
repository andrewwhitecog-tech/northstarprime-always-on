Neri was the youngest roofmaker in Glass City. She saved every useful piece: a blue corner, a gold hinge, a violet pane no bigger than her palm. “A roof,” Neri said, “is a promise that the sky may knock without coming in.”

Spread 2

One evening, the gardeners brought ten moonseeds to the courtyard. Each seed glowed like a drop of milk under starlight. “I can shelter them,” said Neri. “Glass-rain before midnight,” warned the weather bell. The gardeners hurried to cover the tall flowers. The ten new seeds had no leaves, no roots, and no roofs.

For the first moonseed, Neri raised ONE little roof. It was blue and pointed. One. Two. For the next pair, she raised TWO round roofs. They shone like two quiet moons. The seeds slept under crystal shadows.

For the next three seeds, Neri fitted THREE triangle roofs beside the fountain. One, two, three. One, two, three, four. For the last four seeds, she balanced FOUR square roofs near the warm wall. “Plenty strong,” Neri said. The fountain made a doubtful sound.

Glass-rain clicked against the courtyard. One roof trembled. Two roofs tapped together. Three slid sideways. Soon every little roof rattled on its own little legs. Neri ran from one to the next. There were ten roofs and only two of her hands.

Neri crouched beside the smallest moonseed. Its roof had tipped, but its gold hinge touched the blue corner next door. The two pieces held each other still. Neri looked across the courtyard. “I counted the roofs,” she said. “I forgot to count what they could share.”

Neri carried the roofs into one wide circle. Blue leaned on gold. Gold joined violet. Violet rested against copper. Around they went until ten separate roofs became one spiral canopy. It had ten glowing windows and one strong center.

The glass-rain drummed again. This time, every piece passed the tapping to the next. The canopy bent. The canopy sang. The canopy held. Beneath its ten windows, ten moonseeds opened ten silver leaves. Their light braided into one rainbow.

Spread 12

At dawn, the gardeners found one bright garden where ten small shelters had been. There was room to water every seed. There was room to walk between every bed. There was even room for Neri to lie beneath the leaves and watch the rain slide away. Neri still saved every useful piece. A blue corner. A gold hinge. A violet pane no bigger than her palm. But now, before she built, she asked one more question: “What can this piece lean toward?” And the whole city grew stronger by counting together.
