SHARD 01 THE NIGHT MIRROR At Glasswater Quay, the sea glowed from below and the sky glowed from above. Once each year, the people rolled the great Night Mirror onto the festival pier. Its round black glass caught every lantern in the harbor and returned a thousand colors to the water. Luma was the youngest lens-keeper. She checked the mirror's brass frame, polished its crescent feet, and wore her prism goggles even when everyone said they made her look like a surprised owl.

SHARD 02 METEOR HAIL On the afternoon of the festival, a cloud of tiny meteor stones rattled across the sky. Ping. Ting. TING! The Night Mirror shivered in its frame. One silver crack ran across the black glass, then another, then many more. When the storm passed, five large pieces leaned against one another like petals afraid to fall. The harbor went quiet. “I can mend it,” Luma said, though her hands had begun to shake.

SHARD 03 AS IF NOTHING HAPPENED Luma carried the mirror into the floating work gallery. She measured every edge twice and arranged the pieces in a perfect circle. “A good repair should look as if nothing ever happened,” she told herself. She filled each crack with clear crystal paste. She polished until her arms ached. But when Luma raised a lantern, the mirror returned only a flat white glare. It showed no harbor, no sea, and no stars. It looked unbroken. It also looked empty.

SHARD 04 WHAT PIECES REMEMBER Luma lifted the first piece. Inside it shimmered the baker's very first crooked loaf. The second remembered two sisters waving from opposite boats. The third held a missing-tooth grin. The fourth kept the reflection of a lantern carried through a hard rain. In the fifth piece, Luma saw herself much smaller, holding her grandmother's polishing cloth for the first time. The Night Mirror had not merely reflected the festival. It had remembered everyone who stood before it.

SHARD 05 TOO MUCH POLISH Luma tried polishing one piece again. The old reflection grew pale. She rubbed harder, and the little crooked loaf nearly disappeared. Luma stopped so quickly that the cloth flew from her hand. The cracks were not hiding the mirror's memories. Her perfect polish was. She looked at the five pieces scattered across the bench. “I am sorry,” she whispered. “I was fixing the part that told the truth.”

SHARD 06 COLORS PEOPLE CARRIED Luma went from pier to pier with five small glass bowls. She asked each neighbor for one pinch of color that reminded them of something worth keeping. The baker brought warm amber dust. The boat sisters chose sea-green. The child with the new tooth chose bright violet. A rain walker offered deep blue. For her own bowl, Luma ground a thread from her grandmother's old cyan ribbon. No two colors matched, but every one belonged.

SHARD 07 A REPAIR THAT SHOUTED Luma mixed the colors with liquid starlight and poured them into the cracks. The seams blazed so brightly that every memory vanished behind them. “That is not listening,” Luma said, shielding her eyes. “That is shouting.” She poured the mixture out before it hardened. Then she added one drop of dark sea-glass, one drop of patient rain, and enough clear space for the old reflections to breathe. The new mixture glowed without taking over.

SHARD 08 ROOM BETWEEN PIECES Still, the five pieces would not lie flat. Luma pushed their edges together. They buckled. She clamped them tighter. The brass frame groaned. At last she loosened every clamp. Instead of forcing the pieces to pretend they had never separated, she left a hair-thin space between them. The mirror settled with a soft, deep hum. Luma guided the quiet rainbow mixture through each open seam with her slender solder wand.

SHARD 09 A NEW CONSTELLATION The repaired lines crossed the Night Mirror like a constellation no map had named. Luma turned the frame toward the sky. The amber seam held the crooked loaf. Sea-green joined the two boats. Violet curved around the child's new smile. Deep blue carried the rain lantern safely home. Cyan circled Luma and her grandmother. The mirror had not gone back to what it was. It had become a map of what it had survived.

SHARD 10 THE FESTIVAL REFLECTION The first festival lantern rose while Luma wheeled the Night Mirror onto the pier. People leaned closer when they saw the glowing seams. Then the mirror caught one gold lantern. Across its five pieces, that single light became amber bread, green waves, violet laughter, blue rain, and a cyan ribbon turning in the wind. The whole harbor bloomed across the sea. No one asked Luma to hide the cracks.

SHARD 11 OLD LIGHT, NEW LIGHT All evening, neighbors found their old reflections inside the mirror. They also made new ones. The boat sisters stood together on the same pier. The child with the missing-tooth grin now smiled with a full row. The baker held a loaf that was still a little crooked on purpose. Luma's own reflection appeared beside the cyan seam - older than before, goggles crooked, hands no longer shaking. The Night Mirror remembered change without calling it loss.

SHARD 12 WHAT REPAIR CAN MEAN At dawn, Luma returned the mirror to the floating gallery. She cleaned the brass frame. She checked every glowing seam. She did not polish away a single mark. Above her workbench, she hung a new lens-keeper rule: A repair may hold. A repair may help. A repair may also remember. Outside, the sea carried the last festival colors toward morning. And the Night Mirror kept its cracks, because they had learned how to shine.
