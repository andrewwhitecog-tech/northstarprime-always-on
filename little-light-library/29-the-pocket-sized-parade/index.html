Parade weather

The rain marched down Thimble Lane.

Lulo watched it from Gran's window.

'No parade today,' he said.

Gran moved her chair a little closer.

'Not in the lane, perhaps.'

The first marcher

Lulo found a wooden duck beneath the dresser.

It was small enough to carry in one paw.

He set it on the table.

It looked ready to go somewhere.

A useful wagon

His little cart could carry the duck.

But then who would walk at the front?

Lulo put the duck down again.

The cart would carry something else.

Back to contents ↑

04 / 12

The flags

He folded scraps of colored paper around blunt wooden pegs.

A red flag. A blue flag. A yellow one as bright as a petal.

The cart looked taller already.

Back to contents ↑

05 / 12

A very small band

A wooden thread spool made a drum.

Lulo tapped it with a little wooden peg.

The wooden duck said nothing.

Lulo supplied a quack.

Back to contents ↑

06 / 12

The tall problem

The blue flag leaned against the red one.

The yellow flag leaned against both.

Lulo gave the cart a push.

All three bowed together.

Back to contents ↑

07 / 12

A shorter sky

He set the flags lower in the cart.

Now the cart could roll without bowing.

The yellow one made a sunny corner.

Lulo liked knowing which one it was.

Back to contents ↑

08 / 12

More feet

A wooden rabbit joined the duck.

A cloth mouse came out of Lulo's pocket.

The mouse had to ride. Its legs were rather floppy.

At last the cart had a passenger.

Back to contents ↑

09 / 12

The long way

Lulo pushed the parade around the teapot.

Past the breadboard.

Under Gran's raised knitting.

It took a surprisingly long time to cross one table.

Back to contents ↑

10 / 12

The best place

From her chair, Gran could only see the tallest flag.

Lulo considered the window ledge.

He carried the parade over, one marcher at a time.

The duck went first again.

Back to contents ↑

11 / 12

Here it comes

At window height, the little cart looked splendid.

Lulo tapped the spool. Gran hummed a marching tune.

The duck rolled past her.

Outside, the rain was a very large audience.

Back to contents ↑

12 / 12

The return journey

The parade went all the way to the flowerpot.

Then it turned for home.

Gran hummed the tune backward, or as nearly backward as she could.

The cloth mouse rode both ways.

The end. Or a beginning.

Talk together

  1. Which part of Lulo's parade makes a sound?
  2. Why does he change the tall flag?
  3. Where would a very small parade look enormous?

Lulo's parade grows smaller as it gets closer to its audience. Its journey runs from the kitchen table to the best seat in the house.
