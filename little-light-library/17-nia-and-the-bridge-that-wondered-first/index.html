QUESTION 01 HALF A BRIDGE In Auralis, every bridge knew exactly where it was going. Their brass rails hummed before sunrise, and their crystal paths carried people over the river of stars. Nia was the youngest route-keeper in the city. She polished the direction stones and checked every crossing against the map in her cobalt satchel. One morning, a new bridge had grown from the western tower. It stretched halfway across the sky river - and stopped. Seven diamond-shaped sockets waited dark along its rail.

QUESTION 02 THE FIRST QUESTION Nia stepped onto the unfinished bridge. The first socket rang like a tiny glass bell. “What do you notice when everyone else hurries?” asked a warm voice from inside the rail. Nia had never seen that question on a route map. She looked down at the city waking below. “I notice the small sounds,” she said. “Broom bristles. Window latches. The breath before a song.” The first socket filled with turquoise light. One new crystal tile unfolded over the stars.

QUESTION 03 FAR-AWAY ANSWER Across the river, an amber spark blinked from the eastern tower. “I notice the windows that stay dark,” said a child's distant voice. “I wonder whether someone inside needs a morning hello.” A second socket glowed amber, and the bridge grew another tile. “Who are you?” Nia called. The rail chimed gently. “Names come after listening.” Nia opened her map. No rule explained a bridge that asked questions, but the blank space ahead no longer felt empty.

QUESTION 04 A POCKETFUL OF BRAVE The third socket asked, “What do you keep because it helps you feel brave?” Nia opened her star-shaped satchel. Inside was a small brass bead from her grandmother's first route-keeper coat. “It reminds me that brave people can still have shaky knees,” Nia said. Far away, the other child held up a folded amber sail. “I keep this from a boat I was too frightened to launch.” Two more tiles opened, bright as sunrise through honey.

QUESTION 05 QUIET IS NOT EMPTY Now Nia was allowed to ask. “What do you wish people understood about you?” The distant child waited so long that Nia nearly filled the silence herself. Instead, she listened. “I wish they knew quiet does not mean empty,” the child finally said. “Sometimes my thoughts need room before they become words.” The fifth socket shone deep blue. “I wish they knew moving fast does not mean I am certain,” Nia answered. “Sometimes I hurry because I am afraid to choose.”

QUESTION 06 NAMES IN THE MIDDLE The bridge carried their questions back and forth. What smell feels like home? Warm cardamom bread. Rain on copper roofs. What name would you give thunder? A drum looking for its band. A cupboard full of falling spoons. Their laughter met above the star river, and the sixth socket woke in a ribbon of green and gold. “I am Vale,” said the child across the gap. “I am Nia,” she replied. Only one dark socket remained between them.

QUESTION 07 WHEN THE BELL FELL Before the bridge could ask its last question, an aurora storm swept between the towers. Wind twisted the gold rails. Rainbow lightning scattered the glowing answers into the sky. The little question-bell tore loose and vanished into the river of stars. The unfinished span shuddered. Six bright sockets dimmed to embers. “Vale?” Nia called. “I am still here,” came the faint reply. But the bridge had lost its voice.

QUESTION 08 LISTENING Nia tried every question she had heard from teachers and guidebooks. What is your favorite color? What job will you do? What is the tallest tower? The bridge did not move. She tried clever questions, funny questions, and questions with answers she already knew. The seventh socket stayed dark. At last Nia closed her map and set down her satchel. She heard rain ticking on crystal. She heard Vale breathing on the other side. The bridge had never grown from perfect questions. It had grown when someone truly wondered.

QUESTION 09 THE BRAVE SPACE Nia leaned over the last safe tile. “Vale,” she called, “will you meet me in the middle, even if I run out of things to say?” For one long moment there was only rain, starlight, and the small brave space after an honest question. Then Vale answered, “Yes. We can be quiet together.” The seventh socket bloomed violet. A final crystal span unfolded from both sides at once, meeting with a clear, joyful tone.

QUESTION 10 HELLO Nia walked from the west. Vale walked from the east, wearing an amber cape and carrying the little folded sail. They reached the center. Neither one delivered a grand speech. Nia's knees were shaky. Vale's words were still finding their room. So they sat beside the seven glowing sockets and watched the storm move away. After a while, Nia said, “Hello.” Vale smiled. “Hello.” It was enough to begin.

QUESTION 11 A BRIDGE FOR LISTENING When Nia and Vale crossed together, they discovered the bridge's real direction. It did not point east or west. Each crystal tile brightened only when one person wondered and another person listened. People came from both towers. They asked about recipes, worries, games, old songs, and dreams too new to have names. Sometimes the bridge filled with laughter. Sometimes it held a comfortable hush. Either way, the seven sockets glowed.

QUESTION 12 THE UNDRAWN ROUTE Nia added the new crossing to her map, but she left its destination blank. “Where does this bridge go?” the other route-keepers asked. “Toward whoever is willing to wonder first,” she said. Every morning, Nia and Vale met above the river of stars. Some days they carried many questions. Some days they carried none. The bridge always made room for both. And Nia learned that friendship often begins before hello - in the quiet, shining moment when someone truly wants to know.
