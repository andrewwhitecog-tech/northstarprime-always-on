The high color

The sunset put a rose-colored patch high on Suri's wall.

She stretched one wing toward it.

The patch stayed just beyond her feathers.

It looked like a place for a tiny window.

A piece of paper

Suri held a sheet of white paper in the light.

The rose color came with it.

She lowered the paper.

Now she could hold a piece of evening at table height.

Back to contents ↑

03 / 12

The paper drawer

In her drawer were thin scraps that let light through.

Amber. Violet. A green almost like a young leaf.

Suri held one up.

The room looked different through it.

Back to contents ↑

04 / 12

An uneven shape

She tore a small round shape from the amber paper.

It was round on one side and rather mountainous on the other.

Suri turned the mountain downward.

Now it looked like a moon with a ruffled chin.

05 / 12

The frame

She pasted thin scraps around the edge of a plain paper frame.

The amber moon found a place among the other scraps.

One corner curled up.

Suri pressed it gently with a clean feather.

Back to contents ↑

06 / 12

A borrowed window

She held her little frame against the real window.

Light came through the thin paper.

The scraps shone as if someone had lit them from inside.

Suri looked at the wall.

Back to contents ↑

07 / 12

Down here

Colored shapes lay on the pale wall.

The amber moon had softened into a warm, blurry glow.

Suri brought her white sheet beneath it.

The glow came down to meet her.

Back to contents ↑

08 / 12

A feather mountain

Her wing crossed the light.

A great feather mountain rose across the paper.

Suri moved it slowly.

The amber glow vanished behind the mountain, then came out again.

Back to contents ↑

09 / 12

The evening moved

While she played, the sun slipped lower.

The colored shapes crept toward the edge of the table.

Suri slid her white sheet after them.

This window liked to wander.

Back to contents ↑

10 / 12

Too far to follow

At last the color left the table altogether.

The amber paper became only amber paper.

Suri held it to the window once more.

Beyond it, the first blue of evening filled the sky.

11 / 12

What stayed

She laid the frame flat to dry.

Its corners were crooked. One scrap had a thumb-sized wrinkle.

Suri remembered the feather mountain.

She smoothed the wrinkle, then decided to leave it.

Back to contents ↑

12 / 12

Beside tomorrow

The little window rested on the sill all night.

It did not shine by itself.

Suri knew that now.

She left her white sheet beside it, ready for whatever light arrived next.

The end. Or a beginning.

Talk together

  1. Where does Suri find the colored light?
  2. What changes when the sun moves?
  3. What does she keep after the colors leave the wall?

Suri cannot make the sunset wait. She can notice what its light does with the things she makes.
