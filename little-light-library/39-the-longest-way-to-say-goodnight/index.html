01 / 12

Almost ready

Koa had brushed his teeth and found his pajamas.

Dad turned the hall lamp low.

'Ready?' he asked.

'Almost,' said Koa.

Almost had several places still to visit.

02 / 12

The bus stop

The red bus waited beside the sofa.

Koa rolled it under the shelf.

'Last stop,' he whispered.

He checked that its wheels were straight.

A bus should be comfortable at the end of a day.

A book with a bookmark

His book lay open on the chair.

He slipped the ribbon between the pages and closed it.

The story could wait in there.

Koa patted the cover, just to let it know.

04 / 12

The round cushion

The sofa cushion had fallen sideways.

He stood it up.

It fell sideways again.

Koa considered this.

'Perhaps that's how you sleep.'

He left it as it was.

05 / 12

At the window

Across the courtyard, windows shone in different colors.

Someone pulled a curtain.

One square went dark.

Koa waved at the other squares.

They were busy being windows and did not wave back.

06 / 12

A quiet bowl

In the kitchen, the cat's bowl made a little click.

The cat was having a late drink.

Koa waited until it finished.

Some goodnights should not interrupt anything.

Back to contents ↑

07 / 12

A tail went past

He offered the cat a goodnight.

The cat rubbed his ankle and walked away.

Its tail brushed his knee.

That seemed to be an answer, although it had no words.

08 / 12

The last corner

Koa reached his bedroom.

Then he remembered the hall.

He looked back around the door.

The lamp made a soft pool on the floor.

'Goodnight to that bit, too.'

Back to contents ↑

09 / 12

Dad waits

Dad sat on the edge of the bed.

'Was it a long journey?'

'Quite long,' Koa said.

He climbed under the blue blanket.

Dad tucked in the corner that always escaped.

Back to contents ↑

10 / 12

One sound left

A bus passed far below the window.

Koa listened until its sound grew small.

Then the apartment creaked.

'That wasn't me,' Dad whispered.

Koa smiled into his pillow.

Back to contents ↑

11 / 12

The small goodnight

There were no more corners to visit.

No wheels to straighten.

Dad touched the blanket once.

Koa said a very small goodnight.

It did not need to go farther than the bed.

Back to contents ↑

12 / 12

Arrived

Dad left the door a little open.

The hall light lay in a narrow line.

Koa's eyes followed it halfway across the floor.

The rest of the journey could wait until morning.

The end. Or a beginning.

Talk together

  1. Which goodnight takes Koa the longest?
  2. What does Dad do while Koa finishes his journey?
  3. Which quiet sounds would you hear in your own room?

Bedtime can be a journey through familiar things. Koa takes his time, and Dad keeps him company until the journey ends.
