A word came back

'Hello!' called Boro.

From the rock face came a smaller hello.

Boro looked behind Nessa.

Nobody stood there.

Someone who agrees

'I'm Boro!'

The hollow sent back Boro.

'I like puddles!'

Puddles came back, too.

'We have a lot in common,' he told Nessa.

Nessa's hands

Nessa clapped once.

A thin clap answered.

'It returns a little of our sound,' she said.

Boro clapped twice and listened for his two small visitors.

The drum

Boro lifted his little drum.

It had come for the walk, too.

He gave it one soft tap.

The hollow tapped after him.

Too many at once

Tap-tap-tap-tap!

Boro hurried through his best drumming.

The sounds ran together against the stone.

He stopped. The last little tap caught up.

Room in the tune

This time he tapped once.

He lifted the beater and waited.

A small sound came back.

A song with spaces

Tap. Wait.

Tap-tap. Wait.

Boro made a tune with spaces in it.

Nessa rocked her paws in time.

The hollow never quite sounded like his drum.

09 / 12

Something new

A wren sang from the thorn bush.

Boro lifted his beater, then lowered it.

He had not made that sound.

He wanted to hear the end.

Back to contents ↑

10 / 12

The way home

Boro carried the drum as they followed the stream.

Water chuckled around a stone.

Grass brushed Nessa's coat.

Boro's satchel buckle clicked against his side.

Back to contents ↑

11 / 12

One last call

At the bend, Boro turned.

'Goodbye!'

A faint goodbye followed them.

He waved even though the hollow could not wave back.

Back to contents ↑

12 / 12

The part he brought home

At home, Boro tapped his drum for Nessa.

He left a space after every phrase.

The room stayed quiet.

Then Nessa tapped her cup with a spoon, and the tune went somewhere new.

Talk together

  1. What sounds does Boro try in the hollow?
  2. Why does he leave a pause after his drum tap?
  3. Which sounds on the walk home are made by someone else?
