Multimodal Receptor Architecture for Dietary Sodium Reduction Without Potassium Chloride: Crystal Hydrodynamics, Proton-Heat Cross-Modality, Kokumi CaSR Allostery, and Retronasal Aroma Compensation

Patients with advanced chronic kidney disease (CKD Stages 3-5 and maintenance dialysis) are advised to restrict dietary sodium (<2.0 g/day per KDIGO 2024), yet commercial potassium chloride (KCl) salt substitutes pose severe hyperkalemia risks (Greer et al., Hypertension, 2020) and activate bitter receptors TAS2R (Sinopoli & Lawless, 2012). This paper synthesizes primary sensory and physiological literature across four non-KCl sodium-reduction mechanisms: (1) salt crystal morphology and spatial contrast, distinguishing volumetric spoon-density differences (47%-53% lower Na mass per tablespoon for hollow flake/hopper pyramids vs. cubic table salt; USDA FDC 173468) from gram-for-gram human sensory reductions (20%-25% via dissolution kinetics, Rama et al., 2013; 28% via surface spatial distribution, Noort et al., 2010); (2) organic acid proton (OTOP1; Tu et al., 2018) and trigeminal capsaicin/piperine (TRPV1; Tominaga et al., 1998) cross-modal central salt enhancement (~2.5 g/day salt reduction in human trials; Li et al., Hypertension, 2017); (3) allosteric activation of the calcium-sensing receptor (CaSR) by gamma-glutamyl peptides in slow-roasted alliums (Ohsu et al., JBC, 2010); and (4) retronasal odor-induced saltiness enhancement (OISE, compensating 20%-30% NaCl reduction; Lawrence et al., 2009).

Precision Clinical Renal Nutrition & Medication Safety in Maintenance Hemodialysis: A Scoping Synthesis of KDOQI 2020, KDIGO 2024, Phosphorus Bioavailability, and Transplant CYP3A4 Guardrails

Synthesizing 53 clinical trials and nephrology practice guidelines (KDOQI 2020, ADA 2026 Section 11, KDIGO 2024, CONVINCE, PIVOTAL), this scoping review formalizes six computational and clinical guardrails for maintenance hemodialysis (CKD Stage 5D) nutrition informatics: (1) preserving 1.0-1.2 g/kg/day high-biological-value protein to prevent protein-energy wasting (PEW); (2) stratifying dietary phosphorus by gastrointestinal bioavailability (20%-40% plant phytate < 40%-60% animal organic < 90%-100% inorganic 'phos' additives; Kalantar-Zadeh et al., CJASN, 2010); (3) enforcing a strict 'Missing != Zero' database integrity rule for undeclared phosphorus in USDA Branded Foods; (4) accounting for culinary potassium-leaching heterogeneity (Martinez-Pineda et al., 2020; Batista et al., 2021) and prohibiting KCl salt substitutes; (5) USDA safe minimum internal cooking temperatures (145°F fish / 165°F poultry); and (6) excluding cannabinoids (THC/CBD) from renal culinary programs because their pharmacokinetics in advanced CKD are poorly studied, they are not expected to be meaningfully removed by dialysis (high protein binding), and cannabidiol (CBD) increased tacrolimus exposure 3.1-fold (AUC) and 4.2-fold (Cmax) in a Phase I trial (Clin Pharmacol Ther 2025; PMID 39601108; doi:10.1002/cpt.3504).
