A plan for blue

Leni wanted to paint the sky above her street.

She put dark blue in a dish.

The sky outside was much lighter.

Leni looked from the window to the paint and back again.

A little white

She added white and stirred.

A pale ribbon wound through the blue.

For a moment, the dish held two colors.

Then the ribbon disappeared into one.

03 / 12

The other brush

Her brush touched a small spot of yellow.

Leni had forgotten it was there.

The next swirl looked different.

She stopped stirring and bent closer to see what had happened.

04 / 12

Not the sky

She made a stripe on spare paper.

It was blue, but not quite the window's blue.

'Almost-sky,' she said.

The name sounded as if the color had made a mistake.

It hadn't.

05 / 12

A few possibilities

She thought of a swimming pool.

She thought of the inside of a shell.

She had no pool or shell on the table.

Only a stripe that would not choose between them.

Back to contents ↑

06 / 12

An idea on the wall

A pale mark crossed the plaster near the window.

It came from light passing through a green glass bottle.

Leni held her test beside it.

The colors were cousins, perhaps, but not twins.

Back to contents ↑

07 / 12

The first new mark

She put fresh paper on the table.

Instead of a sky, she painted a long stripe.

Halfway across, her sleeve nudged the paper.

The stripe took a sudden bend.

Back to contents ↑

08 / 12

A place to turn

Leni followed the bend with her eyes.

It looked like water going around something.

She painted a small brown bank beside it.

Now the stripe had somewhere to go.

Back to contents ↑

09 / 12

The new country

She added a bridge with room underneath.

Then a yellow field.

The water curved past both of them.

Leni turned the paper a little. Her new country was wider that way.

Back to contents ↑

10 / 12

The right amount

There was a little mixed paint left.

She made the river wider near the bottom of the page.

Then she stopped.

The white places in the picture needed some room too.

Back to contents ↑

11 / 12

Trying the name

'River-round-the-corner blue,' Leni said.

It was a long name for one dish of paint.

She said it again while she carried the picture to the shelf.

It fitted better than almost-sky.

Back to contents ↑

12 / 12

Another morning

Outside, the real sky was changing.

Leni's river stayed its new color.

Tomorrow she might mix something different.

Tonight there was a little country drying on the shelf, and the water knew its way.

The end. Or a beginning.

Talk together

  1. Why does Leni change her mind about the color's name?
  2. What does the bent stripe become?
  3. What name would you give her finished color?

A color does not have to match its first name. Leni uses observation and a small accident to discover what her mixed paint can do.
