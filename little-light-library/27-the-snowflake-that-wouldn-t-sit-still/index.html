01 / 12

A clean page

Tess opened her blue sketchbook to a clean page.

She had drawn the apple tree yesterday.

Today she would draw something smaller.

A snowflake landed on her mitten.

Back to contents ↑

02 / 12

Hold still

The snowflake was shaped like a tiny branching star.

'Hold still,' Tess whispered.

She found the sharp end of her pencil.

The flake had already changed.

Back to contents ↑

03 / 12

A very small puddle

She made one line. Then another.

When she looked back, the snowflake was a wet spot.

Tess frowned at her unfinished star.

It was a very short sitting.

Back to contents ↑

04 / 12

Another visitor

A second flake settled on the stone wall.

Tess hurried over.

This one was different.

She turned to a new page, but the wind turned two pages more.

Back to contents ↑

05 / 12

A useful stone

Papa brought a smooth stone for the corner of the book.

The pages stayed down.

The snowflakes did not.

'Perhaps they haven't time for portraits,' Tess said.

Back to contents ↑

06 / 12

What passed

She drew a short white-looking space between two dark lines.

That was where the flake had been.

Beside it she drew a crooked streak.

That was where it had gone.

Back to contents ↑

07 / 12

The tree shook

A sparrow shook the apple branch.

Snow dropped in a soft tumbling cloud.

Tess laughed and drew a great scribble.

It almost fell off the edge of the page.

Back to contents ↑

08 / 12

Tracks

Across the garden ran a line of little footprints.

The sparrow had walked there before it flew.

Tess drew the tracks.

She left a space where the flying began.

Back to contents ↑

09 / 12

The sound page

Papa brushed snow from the step.

Shuff. Shuff.

Tess made broad soft stripes.

'What's that?' he asked.

'Your broom, before it stops sounding like that.'

Back to contents ↑

10 / 12

Come inside

Her pencil grew slippery in her mitten.

Tess closed the book and went inside.

A wet mark darkened the cover.

She carried that in, too.

Back to contents ↑

11 / 12

The morning on the table

They spread the pages on the kitchen table.

There was a star without enough branches, a scribble, some tracks and the broom's soft shuffling.

Papa looked through them slowly.

Back to contents ↑

12 / 12

A place left open

Tess put the unfinished star beside the window.

Outside, snow fell past the glass.

She did not ask it to stop.

She left the next page open.

The end. Or a beginning.

Talk together

  1. Which part of the morning would you draw first?
  2. What does Tess keep when the snowflake is gone?
  3. Can a picture remind you of a sound?

Tess hopes for a picture that will keep still. Her winter morning gives her a page full of moments instead.
